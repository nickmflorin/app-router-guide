The App Router Guide

How to build with Next.js and React the way they are designed to be used: server-first. Patterns for composition, data fetching, streaming, and the client/server boundary, and the goals behind them.

Why this document exists

The architecture and approach we use now will set the stage for the rest of the product's life. This guide is the shared mental model: what we optimize for on every page, the patterns that get us there, and the signs we've drifted. Read the docs it links to; this is the map, they are the territory.

The thesis

On every page, your job is to separate what can be known at first render from what cannot; then maximize the known, contain the unknown, and decouple the parts. Everything in this guide is in service of one of those three goals. Server-side rendering matters because it makes the "known" surface dramatically larger than a browser-only app can ever make it.

How to read this document

Every practice in this guide is tagged twice: by scope (does it apply to all React, or specifically to Next.js?) and by importance (how much it matters that we follow it).

Scope

ReactApplies to any React application, with or without Next.js.
Next.jsSpecific to, or dramatically amplified by, the App Router.
UI/UXA general UI/UX practice, independent of the framework; usually a cross-team concern.

Importance

CriticalFoundational. Getting this wrong undermines the architecture itself; deviate only with a specific, articulable reason; legitimate exceptions are named where they exist.
Very importantOur default on every page. Occasional exceptions expected; use judgment and be ready to explain the deviation.
ImportantA meaningful improvement worth adopting as habit; lowest ceremony to deviate from.
